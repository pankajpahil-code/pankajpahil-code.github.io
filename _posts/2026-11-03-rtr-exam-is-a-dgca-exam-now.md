If you are planning your RTR(A) around an old WPC exam calendar, stop. That calendar belonged to a
different regulator.

## What changed

**The DGCA has taken the RTR(A) examination over from the WPC wing.** It runs under the *Radio
Telephone Operator (Restricted) Certificate and Licence Rules, 2025*, read with **CAR Section 7,
Series G, Part VI**, which was issued on 27 November 2025 and took effect on 1 December 2025.

The written paper is now part of the DGCA's own exam sessions, and you book your practical slot on
the eGCA portal. Check the current DGCA session notice for dates and centres rather than an old
calendar.

## The two parts

Under the CAR you pass a written examination and then a practical.

- **Written:** 50 multiple-choice questions, computer-based, in English. The pass mark is **70%**,
  and a written pass stays valid for three years.
- **Practical:** about 30 minutes of radiotelephony in a simulated environment. The pass mark is
  **50%**.

Some candidates can apply for exemption from the written paper: pilots of the Indian Air Force,
Navy, Army or Coast Guard with 1,000 hours, holders of a valid RTR(A) certificate issued under the
1954 Wireless Telegraphy rules, and holders of a valid flight radio telephone licence from a
Commonwealth country or the Philippines. The CAR still sends them to the practical.

## What did not change

**The WPC is still India's radio spectrum and apparatus regulator.** It still handles frequency
allocation, clearance for where transmitters and masts may stand, and the one that matters to you:
**the aircraft radio station licence.**

So when your notes mention the WPC in connection with licensing the radio equipment, they are
still right. Do not "correct" them. What changed is who examines **you**. Who licenses **the
radio** did not change.

That is exactly the kind of distinction an examiner uses to separate the candidate who learnt a
sentence from the one who understood how the system fits together.

## The short version

> **RTR(A) is a DGCA exam, under the RTR Rules 2025 and CAR Section 7 Series G Part VI (effective
> 1 December 2025). Written: 50 questions, 70%. Practical: about 30 minutes, 50%. The WPC still
> regulates spectrum and still issues the aircraft radio station licence.**
