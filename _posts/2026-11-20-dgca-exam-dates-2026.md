If you are still working off a screenshot of last year's DGCA calendar, put it away.
The 2026 dates sit in two official documents: the Programme of Examinations 2026, and
the public notice of 22 September 2026.

I have checked both on the [Pariksha portal](https://pariksha.dgca.gov.in), and against
the compiled list on [DGCA exam dates 2026](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-exam-dates),
which was last checked against those documents on 7 November 2026. Where a date is
marked tentative, treat it as tentative. If a later notice on the Pariksha notice
board says something different, the board is right.

## The official calendar for 2026

DGCA's Programme of Examinations 2026 lists four Regular exam sessions and eight
online on-demand (OLOD) sessions for the year, plus the FIR/AFIR oral sessions and
FATA examinations. CPL candidates sit the Regular and the OLOD sessions. The
FIR/AFIR sittings are flight instructor rating orals, not the CPL written papers.

In date order, the calendar gives:

- **Regular sessions:** 10–14 March, 16–20 June, 22–26 September and 15–19 December.
- **OLOD sessions:** 21–23 January, 4–6 February, 22–24 April, 20–22 May, 15–17 July,
  19–21 August, 28–30 October and 18–20 November.
- **FIR/AFIR orals:** 28–30 January, 18–20 February, 18–20 March, 27–29 April,
  13–15 May, 22–24 June, 20–22 July, 12–14 August, 16–18 September, 21–23 October,
  25–27 November and 21–23 December.
- **FATA examinations:** twice a month, once every fortnight. The calendar does not
  list individual FATA dates.

The calendar itself says those dates are tentative and subject to change in case of
gazetted holidays, festivities and reasons beyond the office's control, and tells
candidates to check the notice board on
[pariksha.dgca.gov.in](https://pariksha.dgca.gov.in) for the latest information.

The official Programme of Examinations 2026 PDF on the Pariksha portal has a
9 February 2026 file date. I am writing this on 20 November 2026. Sessions up to
OLOD Exam Session 7/2026 (28–30 October) and FIR/AFIR Session 10/2026 (21–23
October) have already been held. Both the calendar and the September notice give
OLOD Exam Session 8/2026 as 18–20 November 2026. Confirm on the notice board that
a sitting was not moved before you treat any of those dates as final.

## What the 22 September notice added

The public notice — e-file DGCA-19033/1/2022-CEO-DGCA, dated 22 September 2026 — is
the document that opened the last three flight-crew written windows of 2026. It
covers AME, Flight Crew and RTR (Restricted). For Flight Crew it gave these
application and exam dates:

| Session | Applications open | Exam dates |
|---|---|---|
| OLODE Session 07 of 2026 | 23 September 2026 to 8 October 2026 | 28–30 October 2026 |
| OLODE Session 08 of 2026 | 25 September 2026 to 2 November 2026 | 18–20 November 2026 |
| Regular Session 04 of 2026 | 28 September 2026 to 15 November 2026 | 15–19 December 2026 (tentative) |

The notice uses the spelling OLODE for the same online on-demand sessions the
calendar labels OLOD.

The December exam dates for Regular Session 04 are marked tentative in that notice.

OLODE centres in the notice: New Delhi, Noida, Gurugram, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata,
Lucknow, Bhopal, Hyderabad, Pune and Bengaluru.

Regular Session 04 centres: those eleven, plus Guwahati, Bhubaneswar, Patna,
Ahmedabad, Chandigarh and Kochi.

In an OLODE session you choose your own date and time from the slots still open, and
those slots are given first come, first served. The same notice says the Technical
Performance paper, and the ATPL oral for a second or third attempt, will not be
opened in those OLODE sessions.

Applications had to be submitted on the Pariksha portal by 22:00 hours on the
closing date. The Regular Session 04 application window closed on 15 November 2026.
If you applied, watch the notice board for the admit card, the venue and any change
to the tentative December dates. If you missed both the OLODE 08 and the Regular 04
windows, the 2026 calendar does not list another Regular or OLOD written session
after December. Wait for the next official notice rather than trusting a date that
has not been published.

Still ahead on the calendar after 20 November 2026, subject to the usual caveat:
FIR/AFIR Session 11/2026 (25–27 November), Regular Exam 04 of 2026 (15–19 December,
tentative in the notice) and FIR/AFIR Session 12/2026 (21–23 December).

## How to plan the weeks you still have

If you are in Regular Session 04, work backwards from the exam window, not forwards
from whichever chapter you happen to be in. Start with the papers you actually
applied for. Read the [official DGCA CPL syllabus](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-syllabus)
for each one, then spend the remaining weeks on the topics that keep coming back in
the [recalled exam questions](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-exam-questions) and on
timed papers.

A failed paper is not averaged away. The pass mark is 70% in each paper, marks are
not combined across papers, and there is no negative marking, so a blank is a
certain zero — attempt every question. The pattern is set out in the
[DGCA CPL exam pattern](https://ghostaviator.com/guides/dgca-cpl-exam-pattern) guide.

You cannot apply for a session without a DGCA computer number. If you are planning
a later session rather than this December one, get that done first:
[how to apply for a computer number](https://ghostaviator.com/guides/computer-number).

## After you have registered

Once the application is in, check the Pariksha notice board more than once. Admit
cards, venue lists and any change to a tentative date appear there, not in a
forwarded message. The calendar already tells you the December dates can move.

Sit at least one timed paper in the real format before the exam day, not the night
before. The free [mock tests](https://ghostaviator.com/free-dgca-mock-test) and the
[past papers hub](https://ghostaviator.com/past-papers) are there for that. A
revision plan only works if you have practised under the clock.

If a fact on any compiled page, including mine, differs from the notice in front of
you on the portal, the portal is right.
