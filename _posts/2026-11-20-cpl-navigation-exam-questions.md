The DGCA Navigation paper is the one students most often mis-prepare. It is a
composite paper: general navigation, radio aids and instrumentation, one hundred
questions in three hours, drawn from all three. Every year someone arrives having
revised charts and the 1-in-60 rule to a high standard, and then meets a block of
radio-aids questions they never opened. They do not fail because they were weak at
what they studied. They fail because they studied one third of the paper.

That warning is in the [CPL exam pattern](https://ghostaviator.com/guides/dgca-cpl-exam-pattern)
guide. The way to see which topics actually come back is to look at the questions
candidates recalled after the sittings, collected by topic at
[CPL Air Navigation previous-year questions](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-exam-questions/cpl-navigation).

## What comes up most

On that bank, as published on 20 November 2026, there are **949 questions recalled
by candidates from 41 DGCA sittings, 2019 to 2026**. 241 of them came up in three
or more sittings: those are the ones DGCA keeps reusing. The three most-asked
topics — VOR, ADF, DME and bearings; airspeed, Mach and vertical speed; and
performance and V-speeds — carry 24% of everything asked.

The rest of the list, still in the order the page gives, most asked first:

- VOR, ADF, DME and bearings: 109 questions, asked 179 times, 8.8% of the paper
- Airspeed, Mach and vertical speed: 70 questions, asked 156 times, 7.7%
- Performance and V-speeds: 60 questions, asked 152 times, 7.5%
- The ICAO flight plan: 53 questions, asked 142 times, 7%
- Radio waves, radar and SSR: 61 questions, asked 134 times, 6.6%
- Altimeter, pressure altitude and QNH: 53 questions, asked 133 times, 6.5%
- Time, the sun and twilight: 54 questions, asked 131 times, 6.5%
- Gyros and heading instruments: 49 questions, asked 129 times, 6.4%

Those percentages are of the recalled bank, not an official DGCA topic weight. The
questions themselves are candidates' recollections, not official papers, so the
wording can differ from the paper as set. Every answer on the page was checked
against the DGCA CARs, AIP India, ICAO documents or a worked calculation, with
that independent check completed on 17 October 2026. The method is at
[How answers are verified](https://ghostaviator.com/how-answers-are-verified).

If radio aids and V-speeds together are that large a share, your revision plan
cannot be "general navigation first, and the rest if there is time".

## Three questions DGCA has reused

These three are the most repeated questions on that page. Work them yourself
before you read the method.

**1. Compass heading 095, deviation 3 degrees east, variation 5 degrees west. What
is the true heading?** Options: 093, 103, 087, 097.

This one has come up in 16 sittings, from the June 2023 Regular session through
the March 2026 Regular session.

The conversion runs compass to magnetic to true: C-D-M-V-T. East is added, west
is subtracted, when you are going towards True. Compass 095 plus 3 degrees east
deviation is magnetic 098. Magnetic 098 minus 5 degrees west variation is true
**093**.

The usual mistake is to reverse a sign, or to run the conversion in the wrong
direction because the question asked for true and you are used to going the other
way. The chapter that settles variation, deviation and this conversion is
[Earth Magnetism](https://ghostaviator.com/cpl/air-navigation/nav-15/notes).

**2. V1 should be a minimum of — VMCG, VMBE, or VEF?**

Asked in 13 sittings, including sittings in 2026. **V1 may not be less than VMCG**,
the minimum control speed on the ground.

The usual mistake is to reach for a different V-speed you also revised — VR, VMCA,
VMBE — because the definitions sit next to each other in the notes and feel
interchangeable under time pressure. They are not. The multi-engine performance
chapter is
[Performance — Multi-Engine Aeroplanes](https://ghostaviator.com/cpl/air-navigation/nav-8/notes).

**3. An aircraft at FL350 must descend to cross a VOR 85 NM ahead at FL080. Mean
groundspeed in the descent is 340 kt. Minimum rate of descent?** Options: 1,900,
1,600, 1,800 or 1,700 ft/min.

Asked in 12 sittings. Height to lose is 27,000 ft. Time available is 85 / 340
hours, which is 15 minutes. Rate of descent is 27,000 / 15, so **1,800 ft/min**.
Rate of descent is height divided by time, and time is distance divided by
groundspeed.

The usual mistake is to divide the height by the distance, or to use airspeed
where the question gave groundspeed. Distance, speed and time are worked in
[CX-3: Distance, Speed and Time](https://ghostaviator.com/cpl/air-navigation/nav-cx3-5/notes).

For the first and the third of these, the page notes that the question was
recalled without its options, and that the options were written to it. Use the
method, not the letter of an option, when you meet a similar question in the exam.

## How to revise from here

Start with the topics at the top of that list, including the radio-aids and
instrument questions, not only the general-navigation chapters you are already
comfortable with. Airspeed, Mach and vertical speed, the second most-asked topic,
is worked in
[CX-3: True Airspeed and Altitude](https://ghostaviator.com/cpl/air-navigation/nav-cx3-6/notes).
The free chapter notes for the subject are collected at
[Air Navigation notes](https://ghostaviator.com/cpl/air-navigation). The official
topic list is in the
[Air Navigation syllabus](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-syllabus/air-navigation).

Then sit a timed paper. The [past papers hub](https://ghostaviator.com/past-papers)
and the [recalled-question bank](https://ghostaviator.com/dgca-exam-questions/cpl-navigation)
are the right place for that, not a page of untimed browsing the night before.
